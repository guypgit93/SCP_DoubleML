\subsection{Robustness: alternative composite outcome definitions}
\label{sec:appendix-composites}

This paper's main analysis focuses on the official MDCH flag and its item-level decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}). Two additional composite outcomes built from the same ten items, \emph{any deprivation} (at least one item lacking) and \emph{severe deprivation} (more than five items lacking), are also estimated as a robustness check rather than as headline outcomes, since no fixed-count threshold is well established in the literature for defining severity and \citet{nicoriciuelliot2025} argue against arbitrary count thresholds altogether (Section~\ref{sec:lit-measurement}). Table~\ref{tab:stage1_composite_robustness} reports the baseline difference-in-differences results for both composites, under the same three specifications as Table~\ref{tab:stage1_composite}. Any deprivation is significant and directionally consistent with the headline MDCH-flag result throughout; severe deprivation is null, plausibly because it is rare throughout the sample (well under 2 per cent of every country-period cell; Table~\ref{tab:summary_outcomes}) rather than because SCP had no effect on severe cases specifically. At quarterly granularity, any deprivation is also flagged by the Benjamini--Hochberg-corrected Wald test (Table~\ref{tab:parallel_trends_quarterly_wald}, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-quarterly}); severe deprivation remains clean at quarterly level.

\input{Tables/stage1_composite.tex}

The Northern Ireland falsification check (Section~\ref{sec:res-placebo}) also flags one anomaly among these two composites: a significant, positive coefficient on any deprivation (Simple $0.086$, $p=0.035$; Adjusted $0.080$, $p=0.036$), of the opposite sign to Scotland's own (negative) effect, so it does not mimic or contaminate the headline estimate. Severe deprivation and the official MDCH flag both pass this check cleanly on Northern Ireland, as does every outcome on Wales (Table~\ref{tab:placebo_wales_ni}). Any deprivation is also the outcome that shows the least conservative behaviour elsewhere in this paper (Section~\ref{sec:parallel-trends}), consistent with roughly one false positive being unsurprising across the 12 comparator$\times$outcome$\times$specification cells tested here. One candidate real-world explanation, a change to Northern Ireland's own welfare "mitigations" scheme, was checked and ruled out, since that scheme was extended, not curtailed, over this period. The cause of this anomaly remains unresolved, though its opposite sign to Scotland's own effect means it has no bearing on the paper's identification of SCP's effect.

\subsection{Item-stacked OLS difference-in-differences}
\label{sec:appendix-stacked}

As a check on Section~\ref{sec:res-items}'s independent item-by-item regressions, which treat each item's error term as unrelated to the others despite the same child answering all ten deprivation questions (a \citet{moulton1990} problem), the ten items are reshaped into long format and fit as a single joint model with item and year fixed effects, clustering standard errors at household level to reflect this within-child correlation directly. Both a pooled specification (one average effect across all ten items) and an item-interacted specification (one coefficient per item, with a joint Wald test of the null that all ten item-specific effects are zero) are estimated, in both Simple and Adjusted form using the same covariates as Section~\ref{sec:baseline-did}. Table~\ref{tab:stage2_stacked} reports the results in full.

\input{Tables/stage2_stacked.tex}

The pooled effect is small and not significant in either specification ($-0.015$, $p=0.153$ Simple; $-0.015$, $p=0.137$ Adjusted), and the joint Wald test is only marginal (Simple stat$=1.730$, $p=0.068$; Adjusted stat$=1.640$, $p=0.089$), clearing neither the conventional 5\% threshold. Two items stand out in the item-interacted specification: Celebrations ($-0.015$ Simple, $-0.016$ Adjusted, both $p<0.05$) and, more marginally, Holiday away ($-0.060$ Simple, $-0.057$ Adjusted, both $p<0.10$). Comparing this table to Table~\ref{tab:stage1_items}'s independent-regression estimates reveals a genuine methodological divergence rather than a covariate mismatch, since both tables share identical covariates: Celebrations is robust to the choice between the two approaches (independent: $-0.025$, BH-significant; stacked: $-0.016$, $p=0.043$), but Warm coat is not -- it survives Benjamini--Hochberg correction under independent regressions ($p=0.002$) yet is not significant in the pooled, household-clustered model ($p=0.264$). Since the only thing that differs between the two specifications is whether the ten items are fit as independent regressions or as one joint model accounting for within-child correlation across items, this divergence is a genuine open question about which is the more appropriate way to test a single item's effect when the same respondent answers all ten questions, rather than an artefact of specification choice.
