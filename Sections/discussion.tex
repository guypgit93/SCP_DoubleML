\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Causal interpretation and economic significance}
\label{sec:disc-causal}

This paper interprets its central estimate causally: subject to the identification assumptions defended in Section~\ref{sec:identification-assumptions} -- correct treatment allocation, no anticipation, and no spillovers -- together with the parallel-trends evidence in Section~\ref{sec:parallel-trends}, the Scotland$\times$Post coefficient is read as SCP's intention-to-treat effect on child material deprivation, not merely an association. The specification ladder assembled in Section~\ref{sec:res-dml} supports this reading: moving from Simple OLS ($-0.062$) through Adjusted OLS ($-0.076$) to four DML cells ($-0.028$ to $-0.045$), the sign and rough order of magnitude survive every change to functional form and covariate breadth. If the OLS estimate were instead an artefact of a specific, misspecified linear relationship between covariates and deprivation, replacing that relationship with lasso or random forest -- estimators sharing no assumption about functional form with OLS or each other -- would be expected to produce noisy or sign-inconsistent results, not a still-negative, still-clustered estimate. The OLS-to-DML gap is nonetheless informative in its own right: OLS's covariate adjustment appears to overstate the effect relative to every DML cell, consistent with some genuine nonlinearity in how the six CASE controls relate to deprivation that a linear model cannot capture.

Read literally, the Adjusted OLS estimate implies material deprivation fell by 7.6 percentage points across Scotland's child population as a result of SCP; applied to Scotland's roughly 900,000 children under 16 (16.2 per cent of Scotland's 5.55 million population; \citep{nrs2025}), this is approximately 68,000 children. The doubly robust random-forest estimate under the wide covariate set ($-4.5$ percentage points) -- preferred here over the lean-covariate random-forest cell ($-4.0$ points) because it is significant at the conventional 5 per cent level rather than only 10 per cent, and tests sensitivity to the fuller covariate set rather than just the six CASE controls (Section~\ref{sec:res-dml}) -- implies a smaller figure of around 40,500. Both are back-of-envelope translations of a percentage-point coefficient into a headcount, not an administrative estimate, and both describe the intention-to-treat effect on the whole Scottish child population (Section~\ref{sec:identification-assumptions}), including non-recipient households whose deprivation should not move mechanically -- so they likely understate the effect actually experienced by SCP recipients specifically. The DML figure is remarkably close to the Scottish Government's own modelled estimate of around 40,000 children kept out of relative poverty by SCP specifically in 2025--26 \citep{scottishgovernment2025} (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}), while the OLS figure overshoots it by a wide margin, consistent with this paper's own finding that OLS overstates the effect relative to every DML cell (above). The comparison is nonetheless suggestive rather than a like-for-like validation, since the Government's figure describes income poverty, a related but distinct construct from the material deprivation measured here (Section~\ref{sec:lit-measurement}).

\subsection{Reconciling design tensions}
\label{sec:disc-reconciliation}

The quarterly parallel-trends check (Section~\ref{sec:parallel-trends}) flags three series -- any deprivation, Bed/bedroom, and Outdoor play area -- that pass annual-level testing but not quarterly. Two of the three have a plausible, data-specific explanation rather than pointing to a genuine confound. Bed/bedroom is asked only under the pre-FYE2024 question wording and is estimated on a sample roughly a sixth the size of most other items (Table~\ref{tab:stage1_items}); splitting an already-thin sample into 13-week windows leaves individual pre-treatment quarters imprecisely estimated, so a single noisy quarter crossing significance is unsurprising and does not indicate a real differential trend. Any deprivation shows the same fragility elsewhere in this paper: it is also the one outcome with a significant, unexplained placebo coefficient in the Northern Ireland falsification check (Section~\ref{sec:res-placebo}), despite being null on every other outcome and border. As a union of ten binary items, any deprivation compounds whichever single item is noisiest in a given quarter or region, making it structurally the least conservative composite tested in this paper; Benjamini--Hochberg correction controls the expected share of false discoveries across the outcomes tested together, not the probability that any one specific test is a false positive, so an outcome already flagged twice by unrelated robustness exercises is a reasonable candidate for exactly that. Outdoor play area is less easily explained this way: its sample is not thin ($N=46{,}924$) and its annual-level coefficient is small and far from significant ($-0.013$, raw $p=0.499$), so a quarterly-only flag most plausibly reflects a single noisy pre-treatment quarter rather than a genuine trend, though this cannot be confirmed directly and is retained as a residual, unresolved limitation.

Section~\ref{sec:identification-assumptions} carried forward one spillover threat that could not be tested directly: sustained UK-wide attention to SCP shifting England's own counterfactual trajectory through UK Government policy. The clearest such channel identified in this paper is the UK Government's removal of the two-child Universal Credit limit, which the Joseph Rowntree Foundation credits partly to Scotland's example (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}); the change takes effect from April 2026, after this paper's sample window ends (FYE2024), so it cannot itself have contaminated the English control trend behind the results reported here. This does not rule out lower-level media or political spillover earlier in the sample period, which remains untestable within this design and is carried forward as an honest limitation rather than a resolved one.

The item-level results (Section~\ref{sec:res-item-dml}) counsel caution against reading the aggregate effect as evidence of a specific mechanism. Only two items, Warm coat and Celebrations, are ever significant, and only under simple OLS; neither survives any doubly robust re-estimation, and Warm coat is not even robust to whether the ten items are modelled independently or jointly (Section~\ref{sec:res-stacked}). Combined with the aggregate composite's own consistency across estimators, the most defensible reading is that SCP moved material circumstances broadly, in ways too diffuse across the ten measured hardships for any single item to carry a robust, correctable signal on its own, rather than that SCP left specific hardships untouched. Separately, since SCP is not Scotland's only Scotland-only child transfer, some of the attributed effect may reflect Best Start Grant and Best Start Foods rather than SCP in isolation (Section~\ref{sec:identification-assumptions}); the age-restricted robustness check (Section~\ref{sec:res-robustness}), which if anything strengthens on excluding the under-6 cohort where Best Start eligibility is most concentrated, is reassuring but does not fully separate the two, since Best Start payments continue, at a lower rate, past age six.
