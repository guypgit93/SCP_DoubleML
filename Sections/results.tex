\subsection{Baseline difference-in-differences}
\label{sec:res-baseline}
 
Table~\ref{tab:stage1_composite} reports the baseline single-outcome estimates for the official MDCH flag and food insecurity, under the Simple, Adjusted and Extended specifications of Equation~\ref{eq:baseline-did}. MDCH falls by 6.2 percentage points under the Simple specification and by 7.6 percentage points once the six CASE controls are added. The coefficient becomes more significant on adjustment. Food insecurity moves the same way, falling significantly under both the Simple and Adjusted specifications. The Extended specification, which adds the child's own age on top of the CASE controls, barely moves either coefficient. This confirms the result is not sensitive to controlling for the child's own age. Two alternative composite outcome definitions are also estimated as a robustness check, reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-composites} (Table~\ref{tab:stage1_composite_robustness}).

As a check on whether omitting the standalone \texttt{Post} main effect from Equation~\ref{eq:baseline-did} affects the headline result, an exact replication of \citet{andersen2025}'s own Table 3 specification is estimated in full in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:stage1_case_exact}. Both outcomes come back close to their Adjusted-specification estimates above, confirming the standalone \texttt{Post} term makes little difference to the headline result.
 
\subsection{Item-level baseline difference-in-differences}
\label{sec:res-items}
 
Item-by-item estimates of Equation~\ref{eq:baseline-did} under the Adjusted specification, with Benjamini--Hochberg correction applied across the ten resulting tests, are reported in full in Table~\ref{tab:stage1_items}. Only two items survive correction: Warm coat ($-0.007$, BH $p=0.010$) and Celebrations ($-0.025$, BH $p=0.010$). Bed/bedroom has the largest point estimate ($-0.102$) but does not survive even at the raw 10\% level, and is estimated on a substantially thinner sample (around 7,000 observations, versus 45,000 or more for most other items), since it is only asked under the old, pre-FYE2024 question wording. The remaining seven items are not significant at any level.

\subsection{Item-stacked difference-in-differences}
\label{sec:res-stacked}
 
The item-stacked estimates from Section~\ref{sec:item-stacked} -- a pooled specification and an item-interacted specification -- are reported in full in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:stage2_stacked} (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-stacked}). The pooled effect is null in both Simple and Adjusted form, and a joint Wald test across all ten item-specific effects clears neither the conventional 5\% threshold.
 
Two items diverge from their independent-regression estimates (Table~\ref{tab:stage1_items}): Celebrations is robust to whether the ten items are modelled independently or jointly, but Warm coat is not, surviving Benjamini--Hochberg correction under independent regressions yet losing significance in the joint model, most plausibly because the independent specification does not account for the same child answering all ten items (Section~\ref{sec:disc-reconciliation}; full figures in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-stacked}).
 
\subsection{Double/debiased machine learning difference-in-differences}
\label{sec:res-dml}
 
Table~\ref{tab:stage3_dml_composite} reports the ITT estimates for MDCH under every combination of nuisance model and covariate breadth (Section~\ref{sec:dml}), plus a lasso/random-forest ensemble for the lean specification only, since the ensemble did not complete within a feasible runtime for the wide specification.
 
\input{Tables/stage3_dml_composite.tex}
 
Together these estimates form a full specification ladder, running from OLS-simple ($-0.062$) through OLS-adjusted ($-0.076$), DML-lean-lasso ($-0.035$, not significant), DML-lean-random-forest ($-0.040$, $p<0.10$), and DML-wide-lasso ($-0.028$, not significant), to DML-wide-random-forest ($-0.045$, $p<0.05$). Every specification points in the same direction and at a broadly similar magnitude ($-0.028$ to $-0.076$); lasso is consistently the weakest at both covariate breadths, consistent with linear nuisance models understating the effect relative to random forest, while the two random-forest cells are close enough to each other that covariate breadth looks secondary to estimator flexibility here. OLS's adjustment step overstates the effect relative to every DML cell, a gap discussed further in Section~\ref{sec:disc-causal}. A lasso/random-forest ensemble estimated for the lean specification ($-0.041$, $p<0.10$) sits between the two, as expected. The preferred wide-random-forest cell is also refit 20 times under different cross-fitting seeds as a stability check, discussed alongside its results in Section~\ref{sec:disc-causal} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:dmlstability}).
 
\subsection{Item-level and stacked machine-learning DiD}
\label{sec:res-item-dml}
 
Item-level DML estimates (Section~\ref{sec:item-dml}), using the wide covariate set with lasso and random forest nuisance models, are reported in full in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:stage4_dml_items}. Point estimates broadly track the item-stacked OLS-adjusted estimates in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:stage2_stacked}: Celebrations' lasso estimate ($-0.016$) matches OLS almost exactly, and Warm coat's ($-0.007$ lasso, $-0.008$ random forest) sits close to both OLS specifications. But standard errors are wide enough that even these two items, which survive Benjamini--Hochberg correction under independent baseline OLS (Section~\ref{sec:res-items}), fail to clear correction here. None of the ten items is BH-significant under either lasso or random forest. Holiday away comes closest to conventional significance under lasso alone ($-0.056$, raw $p=0.078$), but does not survive the correction either.
 
This is extended in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:stage5_stacked_ml} to a single, jointly estimated model across items, sharing cross-fitted propensity models across items and predicting all item outcomes at once via a multivariate outcome-regression learner (Section~\ref{sec:item-dml}). Bed/bedroom and Playgroup attendance are dropped from this stage, since requiring one consistent complete-case sample across every included item leaves too thin a joint sample if either is retained, leaving eight items on a smaller joint sample than any single item's own Appendix Table~\ref{tab:stage4_dml_items} sample. None of the eight items is BH-significant (all $p_{BH}\geq0.371$). Warm coat, School trip and Outdoor play area tie closest.
 
The more informative pattern is the direction of change relative to the independently fitted lasso estimates in Table~\ref{tab:stage4_dml_items}: Holiday away, the strongest individual-item candidate under independent fitting, weakens sharply under stacking ($-0.056\to-0.023$), while a few other items (Warm coat, Outdoor play area, School trip) move modestly the other way, still nowhere near significance. This instability, in which the item closest to significance depends on whether items are modelled independently or jointly, more plausibly reflects the noise inherent in ranking ten near-null estimates than a genuine item-level effect. Stage 5 also fits a genuinely different estimator than Stage 4's independent regressions (Section~\ref{sec:item-dml}), so some divergence between the two stages is expected on methodological grounds alone. Taken together with Table~\ref{tab:stage1_items}'s independent-OLS estimates, the item-level null finding now holds under three independent estimation strategies (independent lasso, independent random forest, and joint stacked lasso): only two items ever survive multiple-testing correction, and both do so only under simple OLS, not under any form of doubly robust re-estimation.
 
\subsection{Falsification checks: Wales and Northern Ireland}
\label{sec:res-placebo}
 
As a falsification check, the headline specification is re-estimated with Wales, and separately Northern Ireland, as the treated unit against England, at SCP's actual rollout date; since neither nation received SCP, a null coefficient is the reassuring result. Both come back clear for the official MDCH flag, with no coefficient reaching conventional significance (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:placebo_wales_ni}). A supplementary check on two alternative composite outcomes flags two anomalies, one on Northern Ireland and one on Wales, discussed alongside the full falsification results in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-composites}; neither involves the official flag, and neither has any bearing on the paper's identification of SCP's effect.
 
\subsection{Robustness checks}
\label{sec:res-robustness}
 
Three further checks support the baseline design, alongside the Wales/Northern Ireland falsification check above (Section~\ref{sec:res-placebo}): excluding FYE2022, the partial-rollout year, from the pre-period; confirming the official MDCH flag is computed on the same basis in Scotland and England \citep{dwp2026}, ruling out a measurement-consistency confound; and re-estimating on children aged 6--15 only, to separate SCP from Scotland's other child-targeted transfers (Section~\ref{sec:identification-assumptions}). All three come back clear, with full results reported in Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:fye2022_excl} and~\ref{tab:stage1_age_restricted}. A wild-cluster bootstrap on the headline coefficient also confirms the reported household-clustered inference (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:wildboot}). A separate stability check refits the preferred wide-covariate random-forest DML estimate (Section~\ref{sec:res-dml}) 20 times under different cross-fitting seeds; sign holds throughout but the point estimate and its significance vary more than a single run reveals (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:dmlstability}; discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc-causal}).
